# Minerva contact task scene and raw episode capture

The task is to pick up the cube with one gripper, pass it to the other, and release it into the bucket. Helm supplies both arm and gripper coordinates through the existing ArmPlant receiver adapter. The scene does not open USB, SSH, CAN, or physical motor services. The UI integration owns leader connections and explicit recording commands.

## Implemented mechanics and limits

`robot_platform.simulation.task_scene.MinervaTaskScene` uses the generated Minerva model for forward kinematics. The default `collision_proxy` visual mode draws the source collision meshes; `cad` remains available for full CAD visuals. The selected visual mode is recorded in every episode capability manifest. Both modes use identical contact geometry and object physics. Shadows, reflections and offscreen MSAA are disabled for capture; this is an explicit visual approximation, not a measured real-camera appearance model. The whole robot is drawn black and matte, as the real Minerva is (the user, 2026-10-18): every robot geom in both visual modes takes the workcell's named black (0.035, 0.035, 0.04) through a material with no specular highlight, shininess or reflection (`ROBOT_FINISH`, recorded in the capabilities as `robot_finish`). The source model's colours are not used, and the appearance variation leaves the robot alone. Colour only: the nominal trial is bit for bit the same as before (`scripts/sim/trial_trace.py`). A separate MuJoCo model has one dynamically integrated body: a 36 mm, 35 g cube. The robot consists of FK-posed mocap bodies. A table and five-piece open bucket have ordinary rigid contact geometry. ArmPlant motor dynamics are outside this object scene; contact reaction does not load the arms or change motor response.

The source finger mesh is concave. MuJoCo's convex hull fills the open jaw, which prevented a valid pinch in an initial contact probe. The scene therefore splits each finger's paddle into convex pieces of its CAD surface: one per 5 mm slab from the tip, each the hull of the surface points in its slab, 20 per finger. The pieces follow the paddle's taper and chamfers. On a 0.5 mm voxel grid they stick out of the CAD by at most 1.8 mm, 95% of it within 1.0 mm. The paddle ends where the finger's cross-section narrows below 80% of its widest, 99 mm up. Above that, the rack and pinion mount sits inside the gripper and is left out of contact. A box fitted to the CAD vertex bounds of the straight terminal 30 mm still marks the grasp frame for `evaluation.reach`, but collides with nothing. Until 2026-10-17 that box was each finger's only contact, and a cube held higher up passed through the finger. Other robot collision meshes retain their source convex geometry. The capability manifest lists every active collision geometry and records the scene XML hash, MuJoCo version, task dimensions and limitations.

Moving mocap bodies have no generalized velocity. Native MuJoCo `geom_surfacevel`, calculated from finite differences of FK geometry poses, supplies tangential contact velocity. Normal contact still comes from geometry penetration. No attachment, grasp equality, adhesion, cube teleport during manipulation, or synthetic grasp force is used. **MuJoCo 3.15 or newer is required**; older versions fail explicitly rather than falling back to immobile friction surfaces.

Contact friction and cube properties are synthetic assumptions, not measurements. The robot has no coupled dynamics, calibrated finger force, or self-collision response. Robot/table/bucket contact cannot push a kinematic arm back, and the posed arms make no contact with each other. Explicit signed-distance checks record penetration deeper than 2 mm, of the robot into the table or bucket or of one arm into the other. They preserve the offending geometry names, and make the episode ineligible. These runs are never marked `sim2real_validated`. Two later additions change parts of this: the layout can come from a measured workcell file ([SIM_WORKCELL.md](SIM_WORKCELL.md)), and in the evaluation harness the grippers can be driven, stalling on the cube under their drive's torque ([SIM_GRIPPERS.md](SIM_GRIPPERS.md)).

The table is 0.28 m above the source model base. The bucket rim is 0.36 m high. These heights were selected after checking reach within the current source joint limits; an initially considered 0.50 m table was not reachable with downward fingers. Positions are synthetic task settings, not measurements of a real workcell.

## Outcomes

A pickup requires force-bearing contacts on both opposing fingers of one gripper while the cube is above the table for at least 50 ms. A handoff requires simultaneous opposing contacts on both grippers followed by receiving-gripper support and giver release. Bucket placement requires the cube to be inside the bucket, contacting its bottom, released by both grippers and settled for 250 ms. A cube simply initialized or dropped into the bucket does not count as task success. A return to the table or floor after pickup records a dropped-cube failure. Forbidden penetration, of the table or bucket or of one arm into the other, also prevents success.

`reset()` restores cube state, counters and events, generates a new run ID, and keeps the current robot pose. Position the arms clear of the table before resetting and recording. Reset during an active recording is refused so one episode cannot silently span multiple scenes.

## Process and capture interfaces

`TaskSceneView(scene, joint_map, names, directory=..., metadata=..., viewer=True, camera_roles=("right_wrist", "head"), visual_mode="collision_proxy")` owns physics and capture commands in a separate subprocess. A second owned subprocess owns the visible MuJoCo window and renders one exact MuJoCo state at a time, returning pixels through a fixed shared-memory buffer. The controller sends only the latest paired motor sample; its callback never renders, serializes JSON or writes a pipe. A bounded sender thread and status reader handle IPC. The renderer cannot hold the controller or physics interpreter lock. Its interface is:

- `show(q, qcmd=None, source_time=None, authority="human", command_valid=True)`: nonblocking latest paired receiver-snapshot mailbox. Coordinates use the supplied 14-axis motor order and joint map. The adapter must pass q, qcmd and time from the same receiver row and explicitly declare whether that receiver is armed and emitting valid commands. Loss of command validity during a recording aborts it, including a transient loss that might otherwise be overwritten in the latest-sample mailbox.
- `command("reset" | "start_record" | "stop_record", payload=None)`: bounded asynchronous request with an ID. `snapshot()["last_command"]` reports pending, complete or failed.
- `health()`: small callback status containing readiness, availability and recording errors; no geometry/events are copied.
- `snapshot()`: cached task outcome, a bounded recent event tail (the complete history remains in the episode), capability manifest, capture readiness, camera timestamps, recording state and sticky failure information.
- `close()`: ends the scene. An unfinished recording is aborted, never published as complete.

The scene uses 0.5 ms contact steps and interpolates the latest robot pose over its measured source-time interval, preserving substep rounding remainder. Source gaps over 250 ms abort the scene/capture; readiness expires after 250 ms without source progress. These are provisional capture-quality bounds, not validated robot timing limits. Every interpolated moving substep is checked for forbidden penetration of the table or bucket, and of one arm into the other. Rotated world AABBs conservatively remove separated geometry pairs before the unchanged signed-distance test. They are compared body against body first, since a body's bounds hold all of its geoms', then geom against geom. A pair whose two geometry poses are exactly unchanged reuses the previous result; reset clears this cache. This is a sampled visual/object simulation, not a replay of every 400 Hz receiver tick. Capture stores both source receiver time and scene time. Each camera job freezes the exact MuJoCo state, paired receiver coordinates, source/scene timestamps and task state together. Physics continues while that job renders. Only one job may be pending; its 250 ms deadline is checked independently of worker heartbeat. Stop drains it before finalizing, and a newly forbidden contact or dropped cube aborts active capture even if rendering still holds an earlier valid snapshot; reset is refused while an episode owns a pending frame. Camera frames are rendered from that frozen state, with explicit synthetic camera timestamps. The configured 20 Hz is a target: achieved rate comes from stored timestamps and may be lower. Camera/physics source gaps still enforce the 250 ms bound; no backlog is compressed into shorter simulated time. Worker EOF, timeout or death fails capture closed, and native worker stderr is retained in the run directory. Head and wrist camera views are synthetic viewpoints rather than calibrated physical camera extrinsics.

`robot_platform.data.task_capture.TaskEpisodeWriter` is shared by simulation and real collectors. `append` is keyword-only: `q`, `qcmd`, `source_time`, `scene_time`, `images`, `camera_times`, `task_state`, optional `authority` and `torques`. A real collector passes `scene_time=None`, declared publisher timestamps and explicit camera/clock provenance. A publisher timestamp may describe exposure or host read-complete; that stage and exposure-time uncertainty must be explicit. A collector receive timestamp must not silently replace it. This is not exposure synchronization certification. The initial operator profile explicitly records `right_wrist` and `head`, while both arms and both grippers still provide all 14 named axes. The writer validates the exact declared `camera_roles` roster and never fabricates a left-wrist image. An explicitly selected three-camera roster is also supported. Missing torque is NaN plus an explicit validity mask. Missing required coordinates, stale timestamps, dropped queue entries, or capture errors abort the episode.

HDF5 writes occur incrementally in a bounded worker queue. A unique `.partial.hdf5` remains unpublished until explicit `finish()` drains the queue, verifies at least two frames, closes and fsyncs the file, and atomically renames it. `abort(reason)` only signals failure and never publishes. Both methods expose sticky errors via `status()`.

## Raw schema and training boundary

Schema 8 stores the familiar `observations/qpos`, `observations/qcmd`, `observations/images/<camera>` and `actions` arrays, plus `observations/torques`, `observations/torque_valid`, `timestamps/telem`, `timestamps/scene`, `timestamps/scene_valid`, declared camera publisher timestamps, per-frame authority and JSON task state. File metadata declares real/simulation source, clock domains, joint ordering, capabilities, calibration provenance supplied by the adapter, completion and promotion state.

Actions are next-sample qcmd, preserving the old collector's convention. `action_valid` is false on the terminal row and immediately before an authority change so training must not construct a chunk crossing an intervention. Both arms share authority; this interface does not implement mixed human/policy control of separate arms. Training readers must apply that mask across the entire chunk and validate clocks, active units, quality and split provenance.

Completed raw files use `task_episode_*.hdf5`, intentionally outside the legacy `episode_*.hdf5` discovery glob. The old trainer does not understand the new masks or promotion contract. **Raw capture is not automatic training ingestion.** Dataset validation/promotion remains a separate deliverable. `capture_eligible` indicates local capture checks only; `training_promoted` and `sim2real_validated` remain false. Explicitly labelled scripted fixtures are never capture-eligible demonstrations.

## Evidence and reproduction

Run the focused regressions in the worktree's pinned developer environment:

```powershell
.venv-dev/Scripts/python.exe -m pytest tests/unit/test_task_scene.py tests/unit/test_task_capture.py -q
```

`tests/fixtures/minerva_task_contact_path.json` is a frozen offline IK trajectory, with a prepositioned start and an explicit fixture-only gripper mapping. It exercises genuine pickup, overlapping bimanual contact, release to the other hand, transport, free drop and bucket settling. It does not exercise leader USB, the Rust receiver, ArmPlant tracking, or a safe approach from the hanging pose. The regression checks real MuJoCo forces/events, with no cube pose modifications during the trajectory. The fixture predates the arm-to-arm audit: its handoff passes the two arms through each other (wrists, forearms and fingers, from 2.9 s). So the regression expects contact to find every stage, and the audit to revoke success and eligibility without any penetration of the table or bucket. Negative controls prove bucket contact alone is not success and penetrating the table is ineligible.

Additional capture tests cover atomic publication, both real and simulation clocks, raw paired commands, missing torque, intervention boundaries, stale/missing cameras, malformed coordinates, queue overflow, abort and terminal samples. Rendered three-camera fixture evidence is stored under the ignored `build/task_evidence/` directory when generated locally; it is explicitly marked scripted fixture and excluded from training promotion.


The required `tests/contract/test_task_receiver_capture.py` also runs the actual native receiver and ArmPlant behind scripted dual leader inputs, then the subprocess scene, actual right-wrist/head renderer and atomic episode writer. It verifies exact q/qcmd/source-row pairing, 14 axes, two real rendered image arrays, next-command masks, explicit synthetic/proxy provenance, and failure on worker death. Its deliberately stepped simulation clock is a deterministic correctness check, not wall-clock timing qualification. Linux CI supplies an OSMesa offscreen context rather than skipping this test. Local wall-clock probe results belong to their recorded machine/load conditions and must not be inferred from this contract.


On 2026-10-16, a Windows developer-workstation probe used the production startup order (DISABLED render warmup, then initialize/enable/teleop), continuous `perf_counter`, the existing responsive scheduling hints, the actual native receiver, current verified calibration bindings, and small scripted waveforms on all 14 leader channels. With an unrelated full-CAD browser renderer stopped, the default visible `collision_proxy` scene completed 30.26 seconds of recording: 237 head/right-wrist frames at 320×240, 29.92 seconds of source coverage, 7.89 Hz achieved, and 210 ms maximum frame gap. Across 4,201 controller cycles, the maximum callback duration was 15.78 ms and p95 was 4.85 ms; no receiver or capture fault occurred. The unchanged command and source-gap limits were enforced. This is a local timing result, not a 20 Hz claim or hardware/leader-USB qualification. Earlier runs with the additional browser CAD renderer active exceeded the source-gap bound and correctly left only partial episodes. These observations are consistent with GPU contention; they do not establish performance under arbitrary workstation load.

That diagnostic episode is labelled `scripted_fixture`, moves all 14 axes, has 236 valid nonterminal actions and a masked terminal row, and remains ineligible for demonstration ingestion or sim-to-real promotion. It does not execute the pickup/handoff task. The separate frozen contact trajectory proves the object-contact outcome; neither test substitutes for a complete leader-operated task demonstration. Local evidence is retained under ignored `build/task_loop_browser_closed/` and earlier `build/task_loop_*` directories.
